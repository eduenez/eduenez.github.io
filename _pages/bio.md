## The short version

I am a Mexican mathematician at UTSA. I was trained in analysis and number
theory, drifted into random matrix theory, and have since made my way toward
the foundations of analysis, probability, and computation — with a growing
interest in what all of this has to say about artificial intelligence.

## What I actually think about

If there is a single thread running through my interests, it is a fondness for
**broad, general patterns** — the structures that recur across very different
subjects once you squint at them the right way.

In mathematics, that has meant measure and integration, probability and
information, and the theory of *real-valued* and *continuous* logics: what it
means to reason, and to compute, when truth comes by degrees rather than in
plain yes/no. Continuous logic is a lens that quietly connects analysis,
model theory, and the mathematics underneath modern machine learning, and I
find that connection endlessly worth chasing.

Away from the blackboard, the same taste for general patterns pulls me toward
mathematical physics, economics, logic, and the study of large-scale historical
and social dynamics — historical macrosociology and collapsology more than
conventional narrative history. I read a fair amount of science fiction, enjoy
foreign languages, and like recreational problem-solving for its own sake.

## Teaching

I think of myself as a teacher first. I care about quality education, I like to
push at curricular boundaries and try new pedagogical approaches, and I hold
students to standards I believe they can meet. I am especially glad to work with
students of diverse cultural and ethnic backgrounds, and I take part in
[SACNAS](https://www.sacnas.org/) (the Society for the Advancement of Chicanos/Hispanics
and Native Americans in Science). Much of why I teach the way I do is in my
[acknowledgments](/thanks/).
